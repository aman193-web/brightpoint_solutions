## Details
Meeting Introductions and Agenda: The meeting took place on July 17th, with participants including Kenneth Meraw, Nick Crookshank, Paul Dhaliwal, Amandeep Singh, and a representative from CodeConductor Meetings. The group convened to walk through Figma designs for a product catalog and discuss specific questions regarding the software's functionality (00:04:44).
Assemblies and Items Libraries: Kenneth Meraw clarified the distinction between the "assemblies" library and the "items" library. Assemblies are groups of parts and byproducts, while items consist of individual parts. He emphasized that the user workflow requires these to be handled as separate, distinct menus (00:08:52).
Terminology and Labeling: To better align with industry terminology, Kenneth Meraw requested that the interface labels be updated. Specifically, "categories" should be renamed to "assemblies," and "items" should be renamed to "parts" (00:15:19) (00:19:49).
Navigational Hierarchy: Paul Dhaliwal and Kenneth Meraw discussed the UI structure for assemblies. It was agreed that the "categories" tab would be renamed to "assemblies" to serve as a high-level navigation tool, while retaining the underlying category organization for drilling down into specific assembly groups (00:16:29) (00:19:49).
UI Workflow Concerns: Kenneth Meraw expressed concern about the current prototype's ability to facilitate drag-and-drop actions. He noted that users need to view both the parts library and the assembly builder simultaneously. The CodeConductor Meetings team committed to exploring options to accommodate this workflow (00:20:42).
Bid Builder and Pricing Enhancements: The team reviewed updates to the "Pricing" section, which now includes labor profiles, global labor profile discipline overrides, and categorized labor levels such as electrician level 2 and level 3. The interface also supports viewing cumulative costs for material line items, as demonstrated with a $12,124 total for ten material items (00:23:39).
Collapsible UI Elements: Kenneth Meraw suggested using collapsible dropdowns for sections within the bid builder, such as material and labor categories, to improve navigation. While this functionality was not yet present in the prototype, the team acknowledged that it could be implemented (00:26:51).
Comparison to Industry Software: Kenneth Meraw compared the current prototype to his existing workflow in the McCormick software to illustrate the need for a more professional, flow-oriented design. He noted that while the current prototype is functional, the organization of extensions and pricing requires more structure to be effective (00:29:36) (00:39:49).
Labor Allocation and Crew Management: Kenneth Meraw demonstrated how the bid builder should allow users to assign percentages of a job to specific crew members, such as a foreman, to accurately calculate total hours (00:34:40).
Quote and Subcontractor Management: The discussion covered the integration of quotes and subcontractors into the bid builder. Kenneth Meraw noted that subcontractor work often requires only a price entry rather than associated labor hours, and the system should provide a clear way to manage these costs (00:36:26).
Direct Job Expenses: Kenneth Meraw highlighted the need for a dedicated "direct job expenses" checklist, covering items like demolition, permits, and equipment rental. These items must be easily selectable and editable to ensure clarity in the bidding process (00:37:35).
Global Settings for Markup: Kenneth Meraw advocated for a global settings tab where users can pre-set default markup percentages (e.g., 40% on material and labor). He noted that the system should allow these defaults to be auto-populated while remaining overrideable for specific bids (00:45:53).
User Access Levels: Kenneth Meraw identified the need for distinct user access roles. Estimators require full access, while field staff require a restricted login to view specific project data without the ability to alter the entire bid (00:48:10).
Field Staff Functionality: Kenneth Meraw proposed a feature allowing field staff to generate material lists for specific job segments (e.g., "rough" or "final" materials). This functionality would enable staff to send lists directly to suppliers for pickup without needing to manage the full estimating interface (00:51:21).
Bid Builder Header and Bottom-Line Visualization: Kenneth Meraw recommended moving the summary or "top sheet" to the top of the bid builder list. He suggested placing the markup section directly in the header to allow users to visualize real-time changes to bottom-line totals as they adjust profit and overhead percentages (00:43:28) (00:59:38).
Software Functionality and Pricing Defaults: Kenneth Meraw requested that the software automatically calculate building square footage rather than requiring manual lookups, noting this accuracy is essential. While the square footage and price per square foot should be editable, Kenneth Meraw suggested leaving advanced AI-based sizing training for future development rather than the MVP. CodeConductor Meetings confirmed that pricing and labor defaults can be managed within a settings section, allowing users to override these values as needed (01:03:59).
Parts Library and Material Ordering: The team discussed transitioning from using the term "item" to "parts" within the master item sheet. Kenneth Meraw expressed a preference for allowing users to create lists of materials to order, though they cautioned against making this process redundant when using existing tools like the marquee box to order rough materials. CodeConductor Meetings agreed to consider this to prevent double-ordering (01:06:24).
CRM and Contact Management: Kenneth Meraw praised the implementation of a mini-CRM section, which compiles contact information for project managers and suppliers. They noted that this feature would save time by allowing users to quickly access contact details directly within the job portal rather than searching external sources (01:07:46) (01:18:47).
User Access Levels: The team discussed managing team access to the portal, with CodeConductor Meetings currently implementing four roles: owner, admin, estimator, and viewer. Kenneth Meraw suggested the need for "guardrails," such as ensuring only one user can actively work on a job at a time, though they expressed interest in allowing multiple logins for larger jobs (01:08:50). CodeConductor Meetings noted that server-side management could support multi-user access and potentially facilitate subscription-based upgrades for advanced features (01:11:19).
Company Personalization: CodeConductor Meetings confirmed that users can update company details and logos, which could be tied to specific subscription plans as the platform expands (01:11:19).
Drawing Comparison and Overlay Tools: Kenneth Meraw suggested implementing a compare tool to identify drawing revisions, noting that architects often use clouds to mark changes. While CodeConductor Meetings expressed interest, Kenneth Meraw decided to move the overlay and AI-based comparison function to the "back burner" as a post-MVP priority to avoid unnecessary complexity during the initial rollout (01:12:40) (01:16:17).
Proposal Development and Interface: Amandeep singh noted the renaming of the code section to "proposal sector," which allows users to manage terms and conditions. Kenneth Meraw requested that the interface allow users to view the proposal, including scope language and exclusions, as it is being built. They emphasized the importance of AI pre-filling this information for user review (01:17:32).
Proposal Breakdown and Billing: Kenneth Meraw discussed the proposal breakdown feature, noting that while it is useful for "descoping" and milestone billing, it may require users to manually input costs for specific categories like gear or lighting packages (01:21:24). They suggested that, until the software is in use, this section should remain flexible for project managers to determine how to break down the billing (01:24:10).
Project Status and Bid Guardrails: CodeConductor Meetings and Kenneth Meraw discussed the project status section, including categories like "submitted," "lost," and "won," with a preference for defaulting to an "active" status view (01:25:29) (01:28:16). Kenneth Meraw also requested "guardrails" in the bid builder to prevent users from submitting bids without key information, such as gear quotes or specific material pricing, which would prompt a warning (01:26:44).
Server Storage and Inactive Jobs: Addressing concerns regarding server load, CodeConductor Meetings clarified that thousands of inactive jobs would impact storage but not overall performance. Kenneth Meraw concluded that this approach was acceptable, avoiding the need for a separate desktop application for archiving (01:29:38).
Upcoming Review Schedule: Kenneth Meraw will be away for the next week and suggested scheduling a review meeting for early August, around the 4th, when they return (01:30:47).
Development and Navigation Improvements: The team agreed to conduct at least one more review meeting to refine the functionality of the assemblies builder and items library (01:31:57). Kenneth Meraw emphasized the need for a clearer, condensed interface—possibly using a split screen or toggleable tabs—to allow users to navigate libraries and count items without switching between disparate modules (01:33:17) (01:38:35).
Common Items Accessibility: To improve efficiency, Kenneth Meraw proposed an auto-generated or "commonly used" items folder, allowing users to access frequently used parts and assemblies without navigating through the entire library (01:35:54).
AI-Powered Chat Assistant: Kenneth Meraw advocated for an AI chat feature that could provide project summaries and answer specific questions, such as identifying who supplies light fixtures or confirming specific installation requirements like EMT or MC cabling (01:40:00). CodeConductor Meetings expressed that this is a viable option to implement alongside the existing AI review functionality (01:41:52).
Backlog and Task Tracking: CodeConductor Meetings noted that a backlog of questions and open items has been maintained, with some marked as answered. Kenneth Meraw requested that these items be tracked in a central folder—distinct from email—to help them monitor addressed versus open items effectively (01:42:50).
Website Development and Meeting Times: Nick crookshank agreed to develop concepts for the website during Kenneth Meraw’s absence (01:44:43). To accommodate the time zone differences for the CodeConductor Meetings team, the participants agreed to shift meeting times earlier, moving from 11:00 a.m. Pacific to 9:00 a.m. Pacific (01:45:35).
Communication Channels: The team agreed to use both email and WhatsApp for ongoing communication. Nick crookshank provided a phone number to facilitate the creation of a group chat for easier coordination (01:47:32).
